# Reproducible benchmark methods

**Current model scope (23 October 2026):** Version 0.11.0 bundles the three-input duration-aware estimator. Its complete development, calibration, common-row comparisons and technical examples are specified in [Supplementary Methods](supplementary_methods.md) and [the manuscript evidence audit](evidence/manuscript_20261023/evidence_audit.json). The methods below describe preserved exploratory replay and legacy retrospective analyses; they are not new-model validation. [Historical results](historical_model_results.md) retain their original numerical outputs. The current model assumes production towards configured duration, whereas historical fitting outcomes are observed-ending yields; early stops must be handled explicitly in interpretation.

## Estimand and evidence status

The available outcome is recorded final passed bases, divided by 10^9 to obtain gigabases (Gb). Actual end times and stop reasons are unavailable. This outcome is subject to operator decisions and failures; it is not the counterfactual yield at a common planned duration. Linear extrapolation to planned duration is therefore a transparent comparator with a potentially different operational endpoint. Do not substitute actual final duration into an early predictor. The prospective protocol must distinguish observed-ending yield from yield at a predefined fixed duration and handle early stopping explicitly.

The default run is exploratory retraining on the 171 anonymous replay histories. No serialized bundled predictor, calibration residuals or diagnostic quantiles are used in fitting or scoring. The outputs cannot reproduce the original benchmark or validate the released models. Complete-three-checkpoint selection and unknown relationships between labels remain limitations even with correct within-label splitting.

## Splits and preprocessing

A fixed seed (20261008) permutes anonymous labels into five outer test folds. Within each remaining development set, 25% of groups are set aside for calibration; the rest are used for model fitting. Group assignments are shared across horizons. At each horizon there is one observation per replay label, with no label appearing in fitting, calibration and test simultaneously. Every label is tested once. Training-set summaries, imputation and scaling use fitting rows only. No parameters are selected against the outer test outcomes.

For future verified data, an explicit manifest assigns related-run groups to training, calibration and test. Each run requires timezone-aware start and outcome-availability timestamps and `timing_verified=true`, meaning an investigator has checked that every input was available at its checkpoint. The runner rejects group overlap, missing/extra labels, empty partitions, training outcomes overlapping calibration starts, and calibration outcomes overlapping test starts. The assertion cannot detect false metadata; provenance review remains necessary. Chronological results remain labelled held-out retraining, not automatically independent validation.

## Comparators and models

Related-tool search (16 October 2026): avoid an absolute claim that no nanopore prediction tools exist. The historical minControl component of minoTour enabled yield and pore-decay estimates ([Jain et al., 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/nbt.4060)). PredictION models SARS-CoV-2 coverage/read-count relationships ([Valencia-Valencia et al., 2022](https://peerj.com/articles/14425/)); its task differs from predicting whole-run final passed Gb. The current [MinKNOW guide](https://nanoporetech.com/resources/product-documentation/minknow-documentation/document/experiment-companion-minknow) describes warnings when a data target will not be reached within the run limit, but does not establish an exportable forecast matching this paper's endpoint or uncertainty outputs. Record the installed version and any comparable output before designing a numeric comparison. [BoardION](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8120926/) and [minoTour](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8796371/) are relevant monitoring/workflow comparisons. This bounded search does not establish a first-of-kind claim or verify current installability of historical software.

For the frozen model evaluation, prioritize cumulative-rate and recent-rate extrapolation plus a development-only historical median, using identical checkpoints and available cases. Compare related dashboards by documented functionality; do not assign them artificial forecast errors if they do not emit the same endpoint. These recommendations do not alter an existing frozen protocol or any completed results.

| Method | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Training median | Median final Gb among fitting runs at that horizon |
| Training mean | Mean final Gb among fitting runs at that horizon |
| Linear planned | Observed passed Gb plus cumulative passed Gb/hour × remaining planned hours |
| Recent planned | Observed passed Gb plus most recent passed Gb/hour × remaining planned hours |
| Ridge | Median imputation with missingness indicators, standardization, fixed alpha=100 |
| Extra trees | Median imputation with indicators, 200 trees, minimum leaf size 3, max_features=0.8, one worker, fixed seed |

The 40 numerical inputs are the replay feature-column allow-list. Labels, dates, final yield and actual duration cannot enter the matrix. Missing all-training features remain represented and are imputed to zero by the specified scikit-learn option. These settings define a new exploratory comparator suite, not the original training recipe. Models predict raw Gb and negative predictions are clipped to zero. The training summaries are run-weighted, not biological-group-weighted.

Extrapolation requires finite nonnegative passed bases/rates, positive elapsed time and a known planned duration at least as long as elapsed time. Missing or inconsistent inputs produce no prediction; no planned duration is invented. Score each method on its available rows and also on the intersection of available predictions from all six methods at each horizon. The latter can be smaller and unrepresentative, especially at 30 minutes because recent rates are often missing. Show both denominators; do not select a winner from mismatched cohorts.

## Uncertainty and probabilities

The calibration partition provides signed residuals `log1p(observed Gb) − log1p(predicted Gb)`. Absolute residuals are reduced to the maximum within each related group at each horizon. For nominal coverage c, use order statistic rank `ceil((n_groups+1)*c)` with one-based indexing. If the rank exceeds available groups, return an unbounded interval rather than claim unsupported finite coverage. Transform symmetric log-scale bounds back to Gb and truncate the lower bound at zero. This is split-conformal construction; exchangeability is a condition, not a demonstrated property of the replay or later chronological data. Coverage is horizon-specific, not a simultaneous guarantee across all checkpoints.

Threshold probabilities mix signed calibration residuals with equal total weight per calibration group and Laplace smoothing. They are empirical probability estimates, not guaranteed calibrated probabilities. Default thresholds of 1, 5 and 10 Gb are explicit configuration values; choose future study thresholds before outcomes are examined. Evaluate Brier score and reliability bins; report event counts and avoid interpreting sparsely populated bins as calibration proof.

## Metrics and generated artifacts

Report MAE, RMSE, median absolute error, signed bias and RMSLE by horizon. Avoid MAPE because the data include zero outcomes. Evaluate empirical 80/90/95% interval coverage, mean width and interval score, including counts of unbounded intervals. Conditional group bootstrap MAE intervals use 1,000 resamples of test groups; they do not refit models, capture model-selection uncertainty or correct unknown cross-label dependence. Repeated runs within known groups stay together in bootstrap draws. Do not treat horizon rows as independent replicate runs.

The runner writes predictions (including unavailable predictions), group assignments, metrics and metadata with hashes and versions. `render.py` writes a common-case table, a PDF/350-dpi PNG with point errors, 90% coverage and 120-minute reliability at the first configured threshold, and reliability-bin counts. Reliability uses each method's available probability rows; its denominator differs from common-case error panels. These are exploratory descriptive figures, not manuscript validation figures. Empty comparison sets cause rendering to fail explicitly.

The prospective frozen-model analysis must instead score time-stamped predictions made before outcomes, preserve frozen calibration and predefined thresholds, and use the separately recorded final outcomes. This runner's held-out mode retrains estimators; it must not be described as that prospective frozen-model analysis.

## Separate retrospective report-reconstruction analysis

The 20-run analysis uses `evaluate_retrospective.py`, not the retraining benchmark or prospective evaluator. The investigator declared distinct patients/specimens/libraries and no forecast influence on stopping. The initial declaration of no development use was superseded by recovered training records: 14/20 acquisitions contributed to all three legacy models, and a further case shared a training flowcell. Known reused flow cells define 17 bootstrap groups. Four library kits and configured limits are described; specimen type is not inferred solely from duration or kit, and no causal kit effect is estimated.

Report-native reconstruction keeps 10/30-minute bins, final-report configuration and the deployed zero target-temperature encoding. Explicit chart times link temperature values to the report. Scans and duty/cumulative statistics are bounded by the nominal horizon, and boxplots use report bucket width. No interpolation, outcome-based imputation or substitution of actual/intended duration is performed. No feature is missing at every checkpoint; sporadic missing values enter only the fitted model's existing preprocessing. This fixes a reproducible retrospective definition without asserting equivalence to training or live features.

The existing bundled predictors are compared with cumulative/recent-rate extrapolation to configured duration and development-only constants. Constants and baseline residuals use the earlier replay-only partition rule (seed 20261008; 25% of replay groups for calibration); no study outcomes enter that calculation. Device label MINION_MK1D selects the bundled MinION residual group. The primary endpoint is observed-ending final passed bases divided by 1e9, reconciled with sequencing-summary passed read lengths and counts. S7/S15 intentional stops remain in the main analysis. S15/S16 setting errors are excluded only in the prespecified sensitivity; their inputs are never replaced with intended 3 hours.

Point summaries include MAE, RMSE, median absolute error and signed bias; interval summaries include empirical 80/90/95% coverage, width and interval score. The fixed thresholds are 1/5/10 Gb, with Brier scores and event counts reported descriptively. Per-method denominators retain missing comparator inputs. Paired MAE differences use the intersection with cumulative extrapolation at the same checkpoint. Percentile 95% intervals use 2000 resamples of whole flow-cell groups with replacement (seed 20261008); all runs within a sampled group travel together and the pooled statistic remains run-weighted. Each checkpoint is analyzed separately. Two intentional-stop cases do not provide precise subgroup evidence; no hypothesis tests or causal subgroup claims are made. Empirical-bootstrap coverage intervals can collapse at 0% or 100% when all observed outcomes agree; such intervals must not be read as certainty about population coverage. Coverage plots therefore show descriptive proportions rather than claiming calibration.

The immutable execution freeze records model/input/source/environment hashes before prediction generation. A separate renderer consumes scored outputs and cannot refit predictors. Full private provenance and run-level numerical files stay outside Git; aggregate results and figure hashes are versioned. The legacy results and their limitations are reported in the historical supplement, including the reversal of the early-checkpoint comparison in the configuration-error sensitivity.
